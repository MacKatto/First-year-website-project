Wait, They Were Never Real?

Yes indeed! Erwin Schrödinger, though famously associated with "Schrödinger's cat," never owned a cat, nor did the thought experiment stem from any personal experience with one. 

The "cat" in his theoretical scenario was simply a conceptual device used to highlight the bizarre and counterintuitive implications of quantum mechanics, particularly the idea of superposition and the role of observation in determining the state of a system.

The Schrödinger's Cat Thought Experiment:

The thought experiment imagines a scenario in which a cat is placed in a sealed box containing a radioactive atom, a Geiger counter, poison, and a hammer. If the atom decays, the Geiger counter triggers the hammer to release the poison, killing the cat. If the atom does not decay, the cat remains alive. 

According to quantum mechanics, the atom is in a superposition of decayed and not decayed until observed, meaning the cat, being tied to the atom’s fate, is simultaneously alive and dead. Only when the box is opened does the cat’s state "collapse" into either alive or dead. 

The Purpose:

Schrödinger developed this paradox in 1935 as a critique of the Copenhagen interpretation of quantum mechanics. This interpretation suggests that a quantum particle, like an atom, can exist in all possible states simultaneously until it is measured or observed. Schrödinger sought to demonstrate how absurd this principle might seem when applied to larger, tangible objects in the macroscopic world, such as a cat.

Above is a very simplifed example of showcase of the experiment. Schrödinger cat exemplifies how weird quantum mechanics can be when it comes to observation and reality in a memorable way! 

Why Schrödinger's Cat is Famous Today:

Schrödinger's purpose was not to propose a realistic experiment but to underline the philosophical and interpretational challenges in quantum physics. While the paradox seems strange, it effectively illustrates how quantum mechanics defies classical logic when applied to the macroscopic world.

Over time, the cat has become a widely recognized symbol of quantum physics and its mysteries, though it remains a metaphor rather than a real-life anecdote.
